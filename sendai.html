Sendai

5 – 10 NOV · 5 NIGHTS · BASE 3 OF 5

Sendai is the capital of Miyagi Prefecture and the largest city in the Tohoku region, founded as a castle town in 1600 by the daimyo Date Masamune. Its wide, tree-lined streets earned it the nickname "Mori no Miyako" (City of Trees). It's the trip's longest single stop, used as a base for day trips to Naruko Gorge/Onsen, Matsushima Bay, and Yamadera, plus its own in-town sights.

Naruko Gorge — Naruko

Free · open-air, no gate

  • ~2–2.5km gorge with a 100m-deep cliff; the classic view (Ofukazawa Bridge) is from the observation deck by Narukokyo Resthouse at the western end.
  • A 2008 earthquake destroyed most of the original gorge-bottom trail — only short out-and-back sections remain open on each side (Nakayamadaira side ~350m, Naruko side ~230m).
  • Peak autumn colors run late October–early November.

Narukokyo Resthouse — Naruko

0830–1700 · free entry, pay per item

  • Shop and cafe (light meals, souvenirs) right beside the gorge's main observation deck — the classic Ofukazawa Bridge view.
  • Also the start/end point of the Ofukazawa Walking Trail below, and the main parking access point for this end of the gorge.

Ofukazawa Walking Trail 大深沢遊歩道 — Naruko

Free · open-air, no gate

  • 2.2km loop, ~45 minutes, running from Narukokyo Resthouse to an entrance by Ofukazawa Bridge.
  • A quieter, forested side-valley alternative to the gorge's own short viewing paths, with the river visible ~100m below.
  • Confirmed via japan-guide.com as the correct name — makes a loop starting around Ofukazawa Bridge and ending at Narukokyo Resthouse.

Naruko Onsen — Naruko

Free to walk · Japan Kokeshi Museum ¥500 · an earlier train back is available at 1410

  • Called the "department store of hot springs" — 8–9 of Japan's 11 recognized hot-spring water types are found here, from roughly 400 springs and over 1,000 years of recorded history.
  • Birthplace of Naruko kokeshi dolls (chrysanthemum-pattern painting, a squeak when the head is turned); the Japan Kokeshi Museum (~7,000 dolls) offers hands-on painting.
  • Several free/paid public baths and foot baths are scattered through town, listed individually below.
  • Local snack specialty: kuri dango (chestnut dumplings).

Taki no Yu 滝の湯 — Naruko

0730–2100 (last entry 2030) · ¥150

  • Undiluted (kakenagashi), naturally cloudy, slightly acidic water straight from the source — ~1,000-year history, traditionally linked to Naruko Onsen Shrine below.
  • Current building rebuilt 1974 in Taisho-era style; run by the Taki no Yu Preservation Society. No-frills — no soap, mirrors, or towels for sale.
  • ~10 min walk from JR Naruko-Onsen Station.

Shimojigoku Gensen Foot Spa 足湯 下地獄源泉 — Naruko

Reported hours conflict between sources (roughly 0830–2000) — confirm locally · Free

  • Beside a free public parking lot, ~3–4 min walk from Naruko-Onsen Station.

Waseda Sajikiyu 早稲田桟敷湯 — Naruko

0900–2130 (last entry 2100), no closed days · ¥660

  • Built by 7 Waseda University students in summer 1948, drilling as part of a practical training exercise, when they struck hot spring water.
  • Rebuilt 1998, designed by Waseda University professor Osamu Ishiyama — modern architecture with rest areas, more amenity-rich than the rustic Taki no Yu.
  • ~3 min walk from Naruko-Onsen Station.

Naruko Onsen Shrine 温泉神社 — Naruko

Open shrine grounds · Free

  • Official name is Onsen Jinja (温泉神社); listed in the Engishiki, placing its origin at least in the Heian period.
  • Enshrines Onamuchi-no-Mikoto and Sukunahikona-no-Mikoto, deities associated with hot springs and healing. Founded in response to an 837 CE volcanic eruption — volcanic rocks from that event remain on the grounds.
  • Has a sumo ring tied to a Minamoto no Yoritomo legend; hosts an annual autumn festival with sumo dedication and a hot-spring offering ceremony.
  • ~8 min uphill walk from Naruko-Onsen Station.

Kirin Ichiban Shibori Taste Experience Tour 一番搾りおいしさ実感ツアー · Kirin Beer Factory Sendai

Weekends/holidays only, 1430 start · ~80 min · ¥1,000 (from 1 Oct 2026) · 20+ only

Shiogama Shrine — Matsushima

Daily 0500–1700 · Free (on-site museum ¥200)

  • Believed founded 1,200+ years ago; enshrines deities associated with protecting fishermen, safe childbirth, and safe sea travel.
  • 15 of its buildings date to the Edo period and are designated Important Cultural Properties.
  • Main approach is a steep flight of 200+ stone steps up to vermillion gates and halls (gentler alternate routes also exist).
  • Grounds hold 300+ cherry trees of 20+ varieties, including the shrine-unique "Shiogama-zakura."
Open in Google Maps ↗

Matsushima Bay Boat Tour — Basho Course — Matsushima

~50 min, roughly hourly 0900–1500 · Green class ¥2,100 (2nd class ¥1,500 / 1st class ¥2,900)

  • Retraces the boat journey the haiku poet Matsuo Basho made from Shiogama to Matsushima, recorded in his travelogue "Oku no Hosomichi."
  • Passes close to roughly 17 named islets — including Niojima (wind/wave-eroded into a guardian-statue-like shape) and Kanejima (cave-like tunnels) — through waters as shallow as ~2m.
  • Departs from Marine Gate Shiogama, the passenger terminal at Shiogama Port (also a "Minato Oasis" rest facility with shops and an observation deck).

Godaido Hall — Matsushima

Daily 0830–1630 · Free

  • Enshrines statues of the Five Great Wisdom Kings (Godai Myoo), shown to the public only once every 33 years — next scheduled viewing is 2039.
  • Founded 807 as Bishamondo, renamed Godaido in 828; present structure rebuilt 1604 by Date Masamune.
  • Reached via the vermillion Sukashi-bashi bridge, whose deck planks have gaps you can see the sea through by design.
  • Survived the March 2011 earthquake/tsunami without major damage despite its exposed island position — regarded as a symbol of Matsushima.

Zuiganji Temple — Matsushima

Daily 0830–1700 (shorter Nov–Mar) · ¥1,000

  • Founded 828 as a Tendai-sect temple, converted to Zen in the Kamakura period; rebuilt 1609 by Date Masamune as his family temple.
  • An alternate approach path passes rock-cut caves historically used for monks' meditation, now containing statues.
  • The Main Hall and its attached Kuri (Zen kitchen) are both designated National Treasures.
  • The adjoining Seiryuden museum displays a life-sized armored wooden statue of Date Masamune and the temple's gold-leaf sliding-door paintings.
Open in Google Maps ↗

Entsuin — Matsushima

0900–1600 (to 1530 Dec–Mar) · ¥500

  • Built 1647 next to Zuiganji to house the mausoleum of Date Mitsumune, Date Masamune's grandson, who died at 19.
  • Mausoleum interior is gold-leafed and painted with Western motifs — suit symbols, crosses, and what's described as the oldest known Japanese depiction of a rose — reflecting the Date clan's contact with the West.
  • Grounds include a Western-style Baroque rose garden, a moss/maple garden with a heart-shaped pond, and a dry-landscape rock garden.

Kanrantei Tea House — Matsushima

0830–1630 (to 1600 Dec–Mar) · ¥200

  • A Momoyama-style teahouse said to have been built by Toyotomi Hideyoshi at Fushimi Castle in Kyoto and given to Date Masamune, later relocated here by his son Date Tadamune — reputed to be the largest surviving Momoyama-style teahouse in Japan.
  • Two rooms feature gold-leaf sliding doors that once hosted feudal lords' wives and shogunate envoys.
  • Matcha tea service available for an extra ¥600–800, with a view over Matsushima Bay — the name means "place to view ripples on the water."

Oshima — Matsushima

Free · open-air, no gate

  • A small pine-covered island just off Matsushima's seaside park, connected to the mainland by a short vermillion footbridge.
  • A historic training/retreat site for Zen and ascetic monks — dotted with hand-carved meditation caves and sutra-inscribed rock walls.
  • The connecting bridge was destroyed in the March 2011 tsunami and rebuilt, reopening about two years later.

Yamadera 山寺 · Risshakuji Temple

Apr–Nov 0800–1600 · ¥500 (Treasure House +¥200, separate)

  • Founded 860 CE by the priest Jikaku Daishi (Ennin); official name is Risshakuji — "Yamadera" just means "mountain temple."
  • Konponchudo Hall, the temple's oldest building, reportedly houses a flame transferred from Enryakuji in Kyoto and kept burning since the temple's founding.
  • Poet Matsuo Basho visited in 1689 during his "Narrow Road to the Deep North" journey and composed a famous haiku about stillness here.
  • ~1,000 stone steps to the summit, about a 30-minute climb. Visited in order: Niomon Gate, Kaisando Hall (dedicated to founder Jikaku Daishi), Nokyodo (a sutra-copying hall), and Godaido Hall — an early-1700s open platform cantilevered over the cliff, said to have the best view of the valley (a different building from the Matsushima Godaido above).

Zuihoden 瑞鳳殿

0900–1650, last entry 1630 (shorter Dec–Jan) · ¥570

  • Mausoleum of Date Masamune, the powerful Sendai-domain daimyo of the Edo period; his son and grandson are entombed in similarly ornate nearby mausolea.
  • Built in the ornate Momoyama-period decorative style, with intricate woodwork and vivid multicolor painting.
  • The original 1637 structure was destroyed in the July 1945 air raids on Sendai; the present building is a postwar reconstruction.
  • An on-site museum displays Date family personal artifacts, including excavated items from the original tomb. Massive old cedar trees line the approach.
Open in Google Maps ↗

Sendai Castle 仙台城跡 · Aoba Castle Ruins

Grounds free, no set hours · Exhibition Hall 0900–1620, ¥770 (optional)

  • Built 1600 by Date Masamune atop Mt. Aoba, ~100m above the city, chosen for defensive high ground.
  • The castle building itself is gone — destroyed across the Meiji-era demolitions, an 1882 fire, and the 1945 firebombing of Sendai. What remains is sections of the original stone outer walls and a reconstructed guard turret, no reconstructed main keep.
  • The armor-clad, horseback bronze statue of Date Masamune is the site's best-known photo spot.
  • Panoramic view over central Sendai from the former castle grounds. Reached via Loople Sendai, ~20 min from Sendai Station.

AER Observation Deck

Daily 1000–2000 · Free

  • Free observation floor on the 31st floor of the AER building, ~2-minute walk north of Sendai Station's west exit.
  • Two terraces: west side overlooks Sendai's shopping streets with Mt. Izumigatake and Mt. Taihaku as backdrop; east side faces the Pacific and gives views of Shinkansen trains passing below.
  • Genuinely free with no ticket required — unusual for a city-view deck at this height.

Where to eat

Sendai's two famous specialties — gyutan (grilled beef tongue) and zunda (sweetened edamame paste) — are largely off-limits: gyutan is meat by definition, and most gyutan-shop broths use beef stock. Zunda itself is plant-based and shows up in vegetarian-friendly sweets (zunda mochi, zunda shakes) at cafes and souvenir shops around the station and Ichibancho — a safer way to try the local flavor. The trip's own plan doesn't book specific restaurants for Sendai; dinners are open blocks, usually near Ichibancho.

Mitsui Outlet Park Sendai Port

Daily 1000–2000 · Free entry

  • Largest outlet mall in the Tohoku region, 100+ stores at discounted prices.
  • Food court serves regional Tohoku specialties — Sendai spicy miso ramen, Akita's Hinai-jidori chicken rice bowl, Morioka cold noodles — alongside standard mall dining.
  • Part of the broader Sendai Port recreational redevelopment area, which also includes the Kirin Beer Factory (under "What to see").
  • ~15–30 min on foot from Nakanosakae Station (JR Senseki Line).

Ichibancho Arcade 一番町商店街

Daily 1000–2100 (typical shop hours; public arcade itself has no gate) · Free to walk

  • Established 1954 as the first arcade-style shopping street in Tohoku; today the largest covered arcade in the region, a T-shaped network along Ichibancho and Chuo-dori.
  • Combines several connected arcades — Sun Mall Ichibancho, VLANDOME Ichibancho, Ichibancho 4-chome — plus feeder arcades Hapina Nakakecho, Clis Road, and Marble Road Omachi.
  • Mix ranges from 100-yen shops to upscale fashion, plus restaurants, izakaya, and souvenir shops.
  • Connects on foot to Sendai Station's west exit via the covered arcade route, roughly a 15-minute walk.

Asaichi 仙台朝市 · Sendai Morning Market

Roughly 0700–1700, closed Sundays and national holidays · Free to browse

  • Known locally as "Sendai's kitchen" — fresh seafood and produce from the Sanriku coast and surrounding Tohoku farms, plus prepared-food and flower stalls.
  • A compact market street, only ~100m long, with an old-fashioned, lively atmosphere and long-standing family-run stalls.
  • About a 5-minute walk from Sendai Station's west exit.
