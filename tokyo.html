What to see

Oyahana → Iwadatami Kawakudari Boat Ride — Nagatoro

~0900–1600 Mar–Dec, 1000–1400 Jan–Feb (weather/water-level dependent) · ¥2,000

  • Traditional engineless wooden boats guided downriver by boatmen with poles, not self-paddled.
  • The ~3km "upper course," ending at the Iwadatami pier near Nagatoro Station — mild rapids past pine-lined cliffs, ~15–30 min depending on water level.
  • Best scenery mid-to-late November (autumn colors); winter rides use heated kotatsu tables.

Iwadatami Rock Bed — Nagatoro

Free, open access

  • Flat-topped bed of crystalline schist along the Arakawa River — a National Scenic Site and Natural Monument since 1924.
  • Schist formed ~70 million years ago when seafloor sediment was subducted and metamorphosed under heat/pressure, later exposed by river erosion — part of the Chichibu Geopark.
Open in Google Maps ↗

Iwadatami Shopping Street — Nagatoro

Free to browse

  • ~400m shop street connecting Nagatoro Station to the Iwadatami rock bed, roughly 30 shops.
  • Known for walk-and-eat food — miso potato (fried potato, sweet miso glaze, vegetarian-friendly) and grilled ayu river fish (not vegetarian) — plus sweets and souvenirs.

Hodosan Shrine 寶登山神社 — Nagatoro

Grounds always open · shrine office ~0830–1700 · Free

  • Founded 110 AD; one of Chichibu's three major shrines.
  • Current buildings date to the early Meiji era, Gongen-zukuri style (main hall and worship hall joined under one roof) with vividly colored carvings.
  • 10-minute walk from Nagatoro Station; popular for autumn foliage viewing.
Open in Google Maps ↗

Mount Hodo — Nagatoro

Ropeway 0940–1700 (seasonal) · ¥700 one-way / ¥1,200 round-trip

  • Ropeway covers 832m from base to summit station in ~5 minutes.
  • Summit (497m) has Hodosan Shrine's Okumiya (inner shrine, below), walking trails, a small animal park, and seasonal flower gardens (Roubai wintersweet, Dec–Feb; plum, Feb–Mar).
  • Views toward Mt. Buko, Mt. Ryugami, and Chichibu town.
Open in Google Maps ↗

Hodosan Shrine Okumiya 寶登山神社奥宮 — Nagatoro

Always open (outdoor shrine) · Free

  • The inner/upper shrine, at the summit of Mt. Hodo — reached via the ropeway's summit station or the hiking trail.
  • Same 110 AD founding legend as the main shrine below, said founded by Yamato Takeru.
  • Not on the trip's own schedule — a bonus stop if time allows at the summit.

Chichibu Shrine — Chichibu

0500–2000 summer, 0600–2000 winter · shrine office 0830–1700 · Free

  • Regional tutelary shrine claiming roughly 2,000 years of history; one of Chichibu's three major shrines.
  • Home of the Chichibu Night Festival (Dec 2–3), one of Japan's top three float festivals.
  • Not on the trip's own schedule — near Seibu-Chichibu Station, a bonus stop if time allows.

Edo-Tokyo Museum 江戸東京博物館 — Ryogoku

Closed Mon, Tue–Sun 0930–1730 (Sat till 1930) · ¥800

  • Closed for renovation from April 2022, reopened March 31, 2026 — freshly open again after a nearly 4-year closure, its first major renovation since the original 1993 opening.
  • New renovation by architect Shohei Shigematsu adds large digital screens recreating Edo/Tokyo skies alongside the existing full-scale reconstructions and models.

Ryōgoku Edo NOREN 両国 江戸NOREN — Ryogoku

Daily 1000–2300

  • ~11 shops in the restored 1929 former Ryogoku Station building, with a full-size sumo ring at its center — ties to Kokugikan Arena next door.
  • Shops lean sumo/Edo specialties: chanko nabe, sushi, tempura, yakitori, okonomiyaki, and soba. No dedicated vegetarian stall — soba is the best bet, but traditional tsuyu is usually bonito-dashi based, so ask.

Meiji Jingu 明治神宮 — Harajuku

Grounds sunrise–sunset (Nov–Feb closing ~1600–1620) · Inner Garden ¥500 · Museum ¥1,000

  • Dedicated to the deified spirits of Emperor Meiji and Empress Shoken; completed/dedicated 1920.
  • Surrounded by a man-made forest of ~100,000 trees donated from across Japan.
  • Draws over 3 million visitors for New Year — more than any other shrine or temple in Japan.
  • Shinto wedding processions are commonly visible on weekends.

MoN Takanawa: The Museum of Narratives — Takanawa

1000–2100 (varies by program) · ¥2,500 (under-25 ¥1,500, school-age ¥800)

  • Admission includes an audio guide. Closed every 2nd Tuesday.
  • Praised for striking architecture; also has a foot bath and cultural/dining spaces on-site.
