青森

Aomori

31 OCT – 3 NOV · 3 NIGHTS · BASE 1 OF 5

Aomori City is the northernmost prefectural capital on Honshu, Japan's main island. It was historically a key ferry departure point to Hokkaido, a role that faded after the Seikan Undersea Tunnel opened and budget airlines took over — today it's best known internationally for hosting the Nebuta Matsuri, a summer festival of huge illuminated lantern floats.

Aomori Bay Bridge

Free · outdoor, always open (pedestrian walkway closed in winter)

  • Cable-stayed bridge whose tower and cables form the letter "A" for Aomori.
  • Total span ~1,219–1,290m, opened to full traffic July 1994, cost ~¥27 billion.
  • Walkway near A-FACTORY looks out toward ASPAM and the retired Hakkoda-maru ferry.
  • A separate promenade below, "Aomori Love Bridge," connects to Aoiumi Park — a known local date spot.

A-FACTORY

1000–1900 (per the trip's planning notes) · Free entry

  • Onsite cidre (hard apple cider) factory — watch production through glass windows.
  • Tasting cards let you sample sweet (3%), standard (5%), and dry (7%) cidre.
  • All made from Aomori's own apples — Japan's largest apple-producing prefecture.
  • ~1 min walk from Aomori Station; opened Dec 2010 alongside the Shinkansen's extension here.

Sannai-Maruyama Site

0900–1700 · ¥410 (official site currently lists ¥500 — worth confirming)

  • Japan's largest, best-preserved Jomon-period village site — over 700 excavated structures.
  • Discovered by accident during a land survey for a baseball field.
  • UNESCO World Heritage since 2021, part of the Jomon Prehistoric Sites in Northern Japan.
  • Reconstructed pit dwellings, long houses, and a large tower stand atop the reburied original site.
  • Entry is through the Jomon Jiyukan visitor center — its Jomon Theater and Sanmaru Museum (~1,700 artifacts) include the "Jomon Big Wall," an 18m×6m wall made from ~5,120 collected earthenware pieces.

Aomori Museum of Art

0930–1700 · ¥700

  • White trench-cut building designed to echo the neighboring Sannai-Maruyama dig site.
  • Three huge Marc Chagall ballet-backdrop paintings, shown in a room with 19m ceilings.
  • Nara Yoshitomo's 8.5m-tall "Aomori-Ken" dog statue, plus Munakata Shiko woodblock works.

Hirosaki Castle — Hirosaki

Garden 0900–1700 · Hirosaki Marugoto Tourist Coupon ¥1,000 (covers this + Neputa Village) · ~40 min from Aomori by train

keep interior expected closed to the public

  • Original keep rebuilt 1810 after a 1627 lightning fire — the only Tohoku keep never rebuilt in the modern era.
  • Since 2013 the keep sits ~70m from its original spot for foundation repairs, with a move back scheduled Jul–Nov 2026 — may be underway during this visit.
  • The keep interior is expected to remain closed to the public (reported closed for renovation into the early 2030s) — this visit is for the exterior, grounds, and garden.

Oirase Gorge (Ishigedo → Nenokuchi) — Lake Towada

No fixed hours — outdoor trail · Free · no bus service mid-Nov–Mar

  • Trail runs the scenic upper stretch between Nenokuchi and Ishigedo, ~9km one-way, about 2.5 hours to walk.
  • The sole outlet stream draining Lake Towada, lined with over a dozen waterfalls cascading from the gorge walls.
  • A road runs alongside/crisscrosses the trail for much of the route — not fully secluded, but closed to cars on certain peak-autumn days.

Lake Towada Sightseeing Cruise — Lake Towada

Circular route 0845–1600 (last dep. 1440 from mid-Oct) · ¥1,760 (+¥550 first class) · closed mid-Nov–late Apr

  • Lake Towada is Honshu's largest caldera lake, with two large peninsulas extending nearly a third of the way across it.
  • The peninsulas are hard to appreciate from shore — the sightseeing boat is the best way to see them.
  • Nenokuchi→Yasumiya and Yasumiya→Nenokuchi routes each run a handful of times a day; check the current timetable before planning around a specific departure.

Towada Visitor Center — Lake Towada

0900–1630 · Free

  • Yasumiya is the only developed area on Lake Towada's largely undeveloped shoreline, and this is its information hub.
  • Small nature museum covering the lake's formation (a volcanic caldera) and surrounding ecology.
  • Starting point for exploring Yasumiya on foot — Towada Shrine and the Otome-no-zō Statue are both a short walk away.
Open in Google Maps ↗

Towada Shrine — Lake Towada

Open shrine grounds · Free

  • On the Nakayama Peninsula near Yasumiya; accounts differ on its founding — either 807 CE by the commander Sakanoue no Tamuramaro, or by the Buddhist priest Nansōbō after his travel sandals broke on the lakeshore.
  • Once considered one of the region's two most sacred sites alongside Osorezan, and a center of ascetic (shugendō) mountain practice.
  • A symbol of Tohoku's traditional water-god beliefs; stayed a syncretic Buddhist-Shinto site until the Meiji-era separation of religions (1868–1912).
Open in Google Maps ↗

Otome-no-zō Statue — Lake Towada

Open lakeshore site · Free

  • Bronze statue of two facing women, 2.1m tall, on Gozengahama beach — the final completed work of sculptor and poet Takamura Kōtarō, unveiled 1953.
  • Commissioned for the 15th anniversary of Lake Towada's national park designation, also honoring three figures tied to the park's history.
  • The figures' hands don't quite touch — a deliberate micron-scale gap Takamura left for "spatial tension," not a casting flaw.
